Morse Code Reference Chart

A dot (·) is a short signal; a dash (−) is three times as long. Leave a 3-unit gap between letters and a 7-unit gap between words. (ITU-R M.1677-1 international standard.)

Quick Guide for Parents, Teachers & Scouts

A 45-minute plan that gets an 8-year-old sending their initials in one sitting.

StepWhat to do
Hook (5 min)Flash SOS (··· −−− ···) on a torch or buzzer. Ask what it means and why it's used. The rhythm grabs attention instantly.
Pattern not letter (10 min)Teach only E (·), T (−), A (·−), N (−·). Have them write “TEA” = − · ·− using dots and dashes.
Mnemonics (10 min)Hand out the mnemonic alphabet. Say S as “si-si-si”, O as “OOO-LOONG-HOOLE” until the rhythms stick. Most kids hold 5–7 in one go.
Partner sending (15 min)In pairs with a torch/buzzer + the chart, take turns sending 3-letter words while the partner copies.
Wrap-up (5 min)Each learner sends their initials. Finish with a whole-group SOS as a memory marker.
